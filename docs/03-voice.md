# 3. Voice (FM)

There are two ways to talk by voice: **simplex** (radio to radio) and through a **repeater**. On campus you'll mostly use simplex.

## 3.1 Simplex: radio to radio (what we use on campus)

**Simplex** means your radio talks directly to another radio, on a single frequency. With handhelds the range is **a few miles**, more from a high floor or a hilltop. That easily covers campus.

**W1YU has a few designated simplex frequencies**, already in your radio:

| Channel | Frequency | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **W1YU V1** | 147.555 | **Main club channel.** Leave your radio on this one. |
| **W1YU V2** | 147.435 | Second channel, for moving a longer conversation off V1 |
| **W1YU U1** | 446.050 | A different band. Sometimes works better inside buildings. |

- **Monitor W1YU V1** when your radio is on. That's how you find out who else is on the air, and how others find you.
- **Make contacts there**, then **move to another frequency for a long conversation** (W1YU V2, for example), so the main channel stays free for others.
- **Make your own.** You and your friends can pick your own simplex frequencies to hang out on, or for a class, an event, or another club. Choose one from the FM simplex ranges, **146.40–146.58** or **147.42–147.57 MHz**, and avoid the ones listed as busy in [`frequencies.md`](../frequencies.md#voice-fm-simplex). Leave **146.520** for calling only, since it's the national calling frequency.

## 3.2 Repeaters: talking across the region

A **repeater** is a single station, usually on a hilltop or tall tower, that receives your signal and **rebroadcasts it at much higher power**. It lets a handheld reach **tens of miles** instead of a few. Most repeaters need the right **offset** and **tone** settings, and these are already set on the repeater channels in your radio.

A repeater is **shared**: lots of people across the region are usually listening to it, and only one person can talk at a time.

- **Don't use a repeater for something you can do on simplex.** If you can reach your friend radio to radio, do that, and keep the repeater free for others.
- **Do use it to meet and talk with other hams** in the area. That's what it's for, and local hams are generally happy to hear new voices.
- **Listen first.** Before you transmit on a repeater, tune to it and listen for a while: how people call each other, how long they talk, and how they pause between transmissions. Every repeater has its own culture.

Local repeaters are listed in [`frequencies.md`](../frequencies.md#3-repeaters).

## 3.3 How to talk on the radio

1. **Listen first.** Wait a few seconds to make sure nobody is mid-conversation.
2. **Press and hold PTT, wait half a second, *then* speak.** People often clip their first word.
3. Hold the mic **2–4 inches** from your mouth, slightly to the side, and talk in a normal voice. Don't shout.
4. **Release PTT** when you're done talking. Only one person can talk at a time; while you hold PTT, you can't hear anyone.
5. **Keep transmissions short**, and on a repeater **pause 1–2 seconds** before you reply so others can break in. Repeaters cut you off if you talk too long (often after about 3 minutes).
6. **Say your call sign** at the end of a conversation and at least every 10 minutes during one. Most people also say it when they start.

## 3.4 Example conversations (QSOs)

A conversation between two radio operators is called a **QSO** (say "Q-S-O"). It comes from an old Morse code abbreviation, and hams use it for any contact. In these examples each line is one transmission: one person holds PTT, talks, and lets go. The call signs are made up.

A few phrases you'll hear:

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **"listening"** / **"monitoring"** | "I'm here and available to talk." This is how you start a conversation on FM. |
| **"this is"** | Separates the call sign you're calling from your own |
| **"go ahead"** | "Your turn, I'm listening" |
| **"coming back"** | Replying to someone ("thanks for coming back to me") |
| **"loud and clear"** | Your signal is strong and easy to understand |
| **"73"** (seventy-three) | Best regards. Used when signing off. |
| **"clear"** | "I'm done and leaving the frequency" |

### Simplex: starting a conversation

Let people know you're there by saying your call sign and "listening". Anyone who hears you can answer.

```
KC1ABC: KC1ABC listening on W1YU V1.
KC1XYZ: KC1ABC, this is KC1XYZ.
KC1ABC: KC1XYZ, this is KC1ABC. Thanks for coming back. My name is Alex,
        and I'm at Sterling Library. How are you hearing me?
KC1XYZ: KC1ABC, KC1XYZ. Hi Alex, I'm Sam. You're loud and clear here at
        the engineering building on Hillhouse. I just got my license last
        week. Go ahead.
KC1ABC: Congratulations Sam! Same here, this is my first contact on the
        new radio. Are you coming to the next club meeting?
KC1XYZ: I'll be there. Nice to meet you on the air, Alex. 73.
        KC1ABC, this is KC1XYZ.
KC1ABC: 73 Sam, see you there. KC1XYZ, this is KC1ABC, clear.
KC1XYZ: KC1XYZ clear.
```

### Simplex: calling a friend, then moving to another frequency

Call someone directly by saying their call sign, then yours. If you'll be talking a while, move off the main channel.

```
KC1ABC: KC1DEF, this is KC1ABC.
KC1DEF: KC1ABC, KC1DEF, go ahead.
KC1ABC: Hey Jordan, want to plan Saturday's hike? Let's move to W1YU V2
        so we're not tying up the main channel.
KC1DEF: Sounds good, moving to W1YU V2. KC1DEF.

        (both switch their radios to W1YU V2)

KC1ABC: KC1DEF, this is KC1ABC on V2.
KC1DEF: Got you, loud and clear. So I was thinking we meet at East Rock
        around 10...

        (…the conversation continues. Each of you says your call sign at
        least every 10 minutes…)

KC1DEF: OK, see you at 10. 73, KC1DEF clear.
KC1ABC: 73, KC1ABC clear.
```

### Simplex: a quick radio check

Useful when you're trying a new radio, a new antenna, or a new spot on campus.

```
KC1ABC: This is KC1ABC. Could someone give me a radio check on W1YU V1?
KC1GHI: KC1ABC, this is KC1GHI. You're a little scratchy but readable,
        about 50% noise. Where are you?
KC1ABC: Thanks! I'm in a basement lab in Kline, I'll try near a window.
        How about now?
KC1GHI: Much better, loud and clear now. KC1GHI.
KC1ABC: Thanks for the check. KC1ABC clear.
```

### Repeater: meeting someone new

On a repeater, leave a short pause before each reply. Lots of people are listening, so keep it friendly and not too long.

```
KC1ABC: KC1ABC listening.
N1QRS:  KC1ABC, this is N1QRS.
KC1ABC: N1QRS, this is KC1ABC. Thanks for coming back. My name is Alex,
        I'm a student at Yale, and I'm on a handheld in New Haven.
        How am I getting into the repeater?
N1QRS:  KC1ABC, N1QRS. Hi Alex, I'm Pat in Hamden. You're making it in
        fine, a little noise but solid copy. Welcome to the repeater.
        Is that one of those new Quansheng radios?
KC1ABC: It is! I just got my license. Thanks for the welcome, Pat. I'll
        let you go. 73. N1QRS, this is KC1ABC, clear.
N1QRS:  73 Alex, good to hear you. N1QRS clear.
```

### Repeater: joining a conversation already going on

Wait for a pause between transmissions, then say only your call sign. The people talking will invite you in when they're ready.

```
N1QRS:  ...and the antenna went back up on Saturday. W1TUV, go ahead.
                                              (pause)
KC1ABC: KC1ABC.
W1TUV:  Sounds like we have a visitor. KC1ABC, go ahead, this is W1TUV.
KC1ABC: Thanks. This is Alex, KC1ABC in New Haven. I just wanted to say
        hello. I'm new and I've been listening to the repeater all week.
W1TUV:  Welcome Alex! Pat and I are just talking antennas. Jump in any
        time. N1QRS, back to you. W1TUV.
```

## 3.5 Checking into a net

A **net** is a scheduled on-air meeting run by a **net control** station. They're friendly and a great first contact. When net control asks for check-ins, say your call sign **phonetically**, and only your call sign:

> "Kilo Charlie One Alpha Bravo Charlie."

Then wait to be acknowledged. Local nets are listed in [`frequencies.md`](../frequencies.md).

## 3.7 Voice tips

- If you hear nothing, or voices sound garbled, check that the radio is in **FM** mode (long-press **0** cycles FM → AM → USB → CW).
- If a repeater "kerchunks" (a short burst of noise or a beep) when you un-key, you're getting in. If nothing happens, check the tone and offset.
- **Squelch** silences static when nobody's talking. If you hear constant hiss, raise the squelch in the menu. Too high and you'll miss weak stations.
- Move around: a few feet or a window can make a big difference on UHF/VHF. Indoors, especially in stone and steel buildings, is tough.
